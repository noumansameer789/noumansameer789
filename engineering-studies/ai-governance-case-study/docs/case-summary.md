# Safety governance in the Tempe automated vehicle crash

## Documented findings

The NTSB investigated the fatal collision involving an Uber developmental automated vehicle in Tempe on 18 March 2018. It identified the distracted operator’s failure to monitor as the probable cause. Inadequate risk assessment, operator oversight and safeguards within Uber ATG were contributing factors; the report also addressed the pedestrian’s actions and Arizona’s oversight. The investigation therefore supports examining the whole operating system, not inferring safety from a classifier score alone.

Source: [NTSB HAR-19/03](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/AccidentReports/Reports/HAR1903.pdf), executive summary and section 3.2. These are investigation findings, not this author’s determination of legal liability.

## Portfolio analysis

An assurance review should assign an accountable owner to each hazard, define measurable operating limits and test the recovery path when perception fails. A human fallback needs a realistic response-time budget and evidence of sustained attention. Changes to emergency braking or operator supervision should trigger a documented safety review before further testing.

## Decision record

Before authorising a hypothetical road test, I would ask for a hazard register, scenario-based test evidence, an operator-monitoring plan and a clear stop-test authority. Missing evidence should remain a release blocker. This is an analytical writing sample; it is not a safety certification or the original assessed essay.
